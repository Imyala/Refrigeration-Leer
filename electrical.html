Control Circuit Workshop

A packaged unit that has stopped. A ladder diagram, a meter, and a fault you have to find — safely.

How this works Look, measure where it counts, isolate before ohms, name the fault

The meter only reads where you put it

  1. Look and listen first. Contactor in or out, compressor running, humming or silent, fan turning or not. Thirty seconds at the machine rules out half the list.
  2. Voltage tests, live. Pick two test points on the ladder and read volts. Along a series chain every node up to the open contact reads 230 V to neutral and every node after it reads 0; the one open contact has 230 V across it.
  3. Isolate before anything on ohms. Resistance, capacitance and insulation tests are dead tests: isolate and lock off, prove the tester on a known live source, test for dead, prove the tester again. The order is graded, and a meter on ohms across a live circuit is refused.
  4. Commit. Name the fault. A safety that is open is usually doing its job — the gauges tell you whether the LP switch is faulty or the charge is.
