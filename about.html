Refrigeration Learning Platform

For TAFEs, universities, RTOs and training departments

Start the course Try the simulator

Teach how refrigeration actually behaves — not just how it's drawn

Most refrigeration teaching material is static: diagrams, slides, videos. This platform is a live vapour-compression system students can probe, break and diagnose — wrapped in a structured 64-module course with quizzes, a final exam and a certificate. Five streams take a student from first principles to trade-course depth and on to the end-of-apprenticeship knowledge assessment: a guided core program, two full refrigeration and air-conditioning streams, an electrical principles stream, and a capstone revision stream. It runs in any browser, imports into your LMS as a SCORM package with gradebook reporting, and needs no installation, no server and no student accounts.

Interactive simulator

An animated cycle with real per-refrigerant saturation data: colour-coded state flow, a live P–h diagram, an analog gauge manifold with PT rings, compressor-speed and load sliders, and kPa/bar/psi + °C/°F units.

Fault diagnosis that behaves like the field

Ten faults with realistic gauge signatures — from low charge to leaking compressor valves. Technician Quiz mode hides the fault and makes students read gauges, superheat and subcooling to find it, with scored feedback.

Hands-on Service Bay

Students run the whole gauge-fitting job themselves, from the van in: choose a hose set (four are offered, one is right — the rest fail on pressure rating, condition or missing low-loss ends), prove the gauges read zero, then remove caps, connect and purge hoses, crack valves off the back seat, take readings and pack up as found. The order of work is graded against the eight taught steps — high side on and cracked before the low hose goes on, so one puff purges the manifold and both hoses — and every step done out of turn is marked and explained. Realistic consequences throughout for venting, skipped purges and front-seating a running discharge valve.

System Builder — pipe the circuit yourself

Students drag components from a palette of thirty into a closed loop in flow order, starting at the compressor. A rules engine decides whether that circuit would actually run, and when it would not, says why and where the component belongs instead: it knows the four runs of pipe — discharge, liquid, distribution, suction — and checks both that each accessory is in the right run and that it is on the right side of its neighbours. Findings come back as an error (the system will not run), a warning (it runs, but not where the component does its best work) or a good-practice tip, and every one of them carries the reasoning: a sight glass ahead of the drier is told it is reading moisture the drier has not removed yet. Five briefed scenarios — the simplest system that works, a commercial cool room, a pump-down circuit, a reverse-cycle heat pump and a low-temperature system with capacity protection — plus free build and an "explain this circuit" walk-through of the loop in flow order.

Diagnosis with instruments, not answers

The Fault Diagnosis Workshop hides the fault and the numbers. Students choose what to measure from thirteen points on the schematic — clamp-on probes on the pipe runs and in both airstreams, gauges on the two service valves, a clamp meter on the compressor supply — each with the instrument you would use and why the reading is worth taking. Seven derived values (superheat, total superheat, subcooling, condenser TD, evaporator TD, air split, condenser air rise) appear only once the readings they depend on have actually been taken; until then the page says exactly what is missing and why it is needed. The evidence panel names the classic signatures — high superheat with low subcooling is undercharge, high superheat with high subcooling is a restriction — and scoring rewards not just the right answer but the right method: measuring the four readings that decide most faults beats measuring everything. Sometimes the machine is healthy, and being willing to say so is part of the exercise.

Structured 64-module course

516 lessons across five streams. The core program runs from heat fundamentals through components, refrigerants, diagnosis, repair procedures and safety, closing with the Australia and New Zealand Refrigerant Handling Code of Practice (2025 edition) taught clause by clause. Behind it, Refrigeration 1 and Refrigeration 2 carry the full technical syllabus — principles, every major component, system types, tools, brazing, piping, psychrometrics, load estimating, commissioning, controls — and an Electrical principles stream covers circuit theory, machines, transformers and control circuits. Every lesson ends in a quiz with explanations; lessons deep-link the simulator into the exact scenario being taught. A fifth capstone stream is final-stage revision for the end-of-apprenticeship knowledge assessment: the mandatory electrical tests and their acceptance values, the limits of a restricted electrical licence, exam-condition calculations, commissioning to the standards and drawing a control circuit or piping schematic from a written brief — written practice questions with model answers you reveal after attempting them, all original, not a copy of any real paper.

Exam & certificate

An exam for each stream — two questions per module — plus a whole-program final exam, all drawn randomly from a bank of over 2,000 questions. A fresh paper every attempt, an 80% pass mark, and a printable certificate of completion with a verifiable ID.

Instructor tools

A cohort dashboard that aggregates student progress exports — per-module completion, exam results, CSV download — entirely in the instructor's browser. No student data ever leaves your institution.

LMS-ready (SCORM 1.2)

Import into Moodle, Canvas, Blackboard or D2L as a SCORM activity. Completion and exam scores report to the gradebook, and each learner's progress is stored against their LMS account.
