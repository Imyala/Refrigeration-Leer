# References & Standards Alignment

The course is aligned to Australian regulatory requirements, standards and
the standard Australian trade texts. Every lesson carries a **References &
alignment** panel citing its sources at topic level.

## How alignment works (and what it is not)

- **Publicly available regulatory material** — the Refrigerant Handling Code
  of Practice, ARC licensing information, and legislation — is **paraphrased
  with citation** where lessons state requirements (recovery obligations,
  licence classes, evacuation methods, leak-test rules).
- **Paid, copyrighted publications** — AS/NZS standards and the ARAC
  textbooks — are **cited at topic level only**. The course does not
  reproduce their text, tables or figures. Learners and institutions must
  work from current editions, which RTOs are required to hold.
- Codes and standards **change**. Where a specific number is safety- or
  compliance-critical (test pressures, vacuum depths, charge limits), lessons
  teach the method and the widely used industry values, and direct the
  learner to the current edition of the governing document as the authority.
- No endorsement is implied: ARCtick is the Australian Refrigeration
  Council's scheme; AIRAH, Standards Australia, McGraw-Hill and the ARC have
  not reviewed or endorsed this course.

### Originality statement

The course is **written from** the sources listed below; it reproduces none of
their prose.

- Each source chapter was read in full and its topics, procedures, data and
  worked examples were mapped to a module of lessons. Every lesson was then
  **written from scratch**.
- **Facts are retained, wording is not.** Terminology, definitions, standard
  values, procedures, formulae and numerical data are carried across unchanged
  — they are facts of the trade and must stay correct — but every sentence of
  explanation is new.
- An **automated originality check** compares every lesson against the OCR'd
  text of the sources, looking for runs of matching words. Passages sharing 16
  or more consecutive words were rewritten. After that pass, **the longest
  shared run of prose anywhere in the course is 15 words**, and those runs are
  unavoidable strings of standard technical phrasing (component names, clause
  titles, formula statements).
- No figures, photographs, tables or catalogue data are copied from the
  sources. Diagrams are original SVGs drawn for this course (`js/figures.js`).

#### The capstone stream

The capstone stream revises for an assessment rather than teaching a source
book, so its originality position is stated separately.

- A real, recent knowledge-assessment paper was reviewed **only to establish
  what the assessment covers** — its topics, their depth, and the form the
  answers are wanted in. **None of it was reproduced.**
- **Every practice question in the stream is original**: its own scenario, its
  own equipment, its own numbers and its own wording. The material does not
  name the awarding institution or the paper, and it does not reproduce,
  paraphrase or renumber any real assessment question.
- An **automated check** compared every capstone lesson against the paper's
  text. **The longest shared run anywhere is 8 words**, and both instances are
  unavoidable standard terminology: the AS/NZS 3000 test list, and the phrase
  "at the end of a new refrigeration installation".
- Where a lesson teaches how to read a design-temperature table from
  **AS/NZS 5149.2**, it uses a table of the right *shape* with deliberately
  illustrative values, and tells the learner to read the real figures from the
  current edition of the standard. This is the same policy applied to every
  paid standard cited here: cite at topic level, never reproduce the tables.

`docs/SOURCE_COVERAGE.md` is the audit trail: which source chapter every one
of the 64 modules was written from, how much teaching material came out of it,
how the content was produced, and where the supplied scans had gaps.

## Maintenance

When a new edition of the Code of Practice or a cited standard is published:
search the course content (`js/course*.js` — `course1–3.js` core,
`course101–116.js` Refrigeration 1, `course201–213.js` Refrigeration 2,
`course301–314.js` Electrical, `course401–410.js` Capstone) for the
reference strings above, review the
affected lessons, and update the lesson text, this file and
`docs/SOURCE_COVERAGE.md`. The test suite enforces that every lesson carries
at least one reference.
